# APP Working Group Data Handling Charter

**Version:** 0.3 Draft
**Effective:** on execution by each Member
**Governing law:** DIFC

## 1. Purpose and standing

This Charter governs how Members of the Agentic Process Protocol Working Group ("**WG**") handle personal data received in the course of protocol development. It is a binding instrument between the Interim Custodian and each Member organisation, and doubles as the Article 26 arrangement between them under the EU General Data Protection Regulation ("**GDPR**") for the data flows described in §5.

It sits alongside the WG Terms of Participation, the **APP Contribution Terms** (which adopt the Linux Foundation's Community Specification License 1.0 verbatim as the operative Working Group licence for intellectual-property rights in contributions), and the APP consultation privacy notice.

## 2. Interim custody and intended beneficiary

**Vach AI Limited** (DIFC) acts as **Interim Custodian** of the WG's records and infrastructure. Interim custody is not ownership. The Interim Custodian's role is to host, coordinate, and safeguard the WG's work until the WG matriculates to an independent foundation.

The **intended beneficiary** of this Charter, of the APP Contribution Terms, and of the WG's records is a not-for-profit standards foundation to be designated by resolution of the WG (the "**Successor Foundation**"). The specific Successor Foundation is a WG decision; no matriculation agreement is in place at the effective date of this Charter.

**Matriculation posture.** On matriculation to a Successor Foundation, this Charter will be replaced by the Successor Foundation's equivalent instruments; personal data handled under this Charter continues to be handled at least to the standard set out here, with reviewer protections not diminished by the transition.

On matriculation:

- the Interim Custodian will transfer to the Successor Foundation the WG's records, the consultation infrastructure, and its rights under this Charter, the Contribution Terms, and reviewer consents obtained under the privacy notice, to the extent transferable under applicable law;
- reviewers will receive notice under the privacy notice in advance of the transfer;
- this Charter continues in force with the Successor Foundation substituted for the Interim Custodian, and Members' obligations continue unchanged;
- the Interim Custodian will delete or return WG Personal Data in its custody that is not part of the transferred record, on a timeline agreed with the Successor Foundation.

Nothing in this section prevents the Successor Foundation, once designated, from adopting its own equivalent instruments and superseding this Charter, provided reviewer protections are not diminished.

## 3. Scope

This Charter applies to any personal data that a Member receives, accesses, or observes through the WG's activities, including:

- comment submissions from public reviewers (identity plus comment content);
- attendee lists, minutes, and recordings of WG meetings;
- correspondence conducted through WG channels;
- any pseudonymised or aggregated derivatives of the above.

It does not apply to personal data a Member holds independently of the WG.

## 4. Definitions

- **Reviewer Data** — personal data submitted by a public reviewer through the consultation, including name, email, country/region, organisation, role, and comment content.
- **WG Data** — personal data of Members' personnel generated by WG operation (meeting attendance, authored comments, voting records).
- **WG Personal Data** — Reviewer Data and WG Data collectively.
- **Interim Custodian** — Vach AI Limited, until matriculation.
- **Successor Foundation** — the not-for-profit standards foundation designated by the WG under §2.
- **Convener** — the Interim Custodian, or the Successor Foundation once designated.
- **Member** — an organisation that has executed this Charter and been admitted to the WG.

## 5. Roles: joint controllership (GDPR Article 26)

For the following processing, the Convener and each Member act as **joint controllers** within the meaning of GDPR Article 26:

- receipt, triage, and dissemination of Reviewer Data through the consultation infrastructure to Members for review;
- collective deliberation, consolidated response, and disposition of comments;
- publication of the disposition record and acknowledgments;
- retention of the WG's records as a shared standards output.

The allocation of responsibilities for GDPR compliance in this joint processing is set out in **Schedule 1 (Article 26 Arrangement)**. The essence of that arrangement is published in the APP privacy notice as required by Article 26(2).

For processing outside the scope above — including a Member's internal review workflow, its own retention, its own onward advice to internal stakeholders, and any use permitted under §6 that a Member performs within its own systems — each Member is an **independent controller** and remains solely responsible for its compliance obligations.

Irrespective of this allocation, a reviewer may exercise their rights against the Convener or against any Member. Each recipient must handle the request in accordance with Schedule 1 §4.

## 6. Permitted uses

A Member may use WG Personal Data only to:

- read, review, analyse, and respond to submitted comments;
- deliberate and vote on protocol drafts;
- prepare consolidated responses, disposition tables, and change proposals;
- contribute to protocol documents, minutes, and public records of the WG's work;
- meet the Member's own legal, audit, and regulatory obligations arising from participation.

## 7. Prohibited uses

A Member must not, and must ensure its personnel, contractors, and sub-processors do not:

- **Use Reviewer Data for sales, marketing, prospecting, lead scoring, market intelligence, competitive analysis, or account planning.** This prohibition covers direct use of reviewer identity and any derived signal (for example, that a named organisation submitted comments on a particular topic).
- **Re-share WG Personal Data** outside the Member's organisation, except to professional advisers under equivalent confidentiality obligations, or as required by law.
- **Input WG Personal Data into any model-training pipeline**, whether the Member's own or a third party's. This includes fine-tuning, embedding into a persistent vector store used across customers, or inclusion in a training corpus.
- **Input WG Personal Data into shared or multi-tenant AI systems that retain inputs beyond a session, or that use inputs to improve models across tenants**, unless retention is contractually disabled and evidence of that configuration is available on request.
- **Publish, cite, or attribute** Reviewer Data outside the publication rules in §9 below.
- **Correlate Reviewer Data with the Member's customer records** to identify or profile individuals, unless the reviewer has consented directly to the Member for that specific purpose.

## 8. AI-assisted processing

Members may use AI tooling to support review — clustering, summarisation, translation, drafting responses — provided:

- inputs are not retained by the tooling provider beyond the immediate session or are protected by an equivalent commercial arrangement;
- outputs are treated as drafts subject to human review before use in WG deliberation;
- the use is disclosed on request to the Convener.

The Convener will maintain and publish a register of AI tooling used in central WG operations.

## 9. Attribution and publication

Reviewer choices at submission govern attribution. Members must honour a reviewer's choice of **public**, **pseudonymous**, or **anonymous** submission in any WG output.

- **Public** — reviewer name and (if given) organisation may appear alongside the comment in public disposition tables and acknowledgments.
- **Pseudonymous** — a reviewer identifier (for example, `R-2026-0123`) may appear; name and organisation may not.
- **Anonymous** — no reviewer identifier appears; the comment stands on its content.

Where a reviewer changes their attribution choice within the retention window, the Convener will update the published record within a reasonable period and notify Members to update their internal records accordingly.

WG member personnel who author comments in their WG capacity are attributed by name and organisation in minutes and disposition tables, unless the WG agrees otherwise in a specific instance.

## 10. Conflict of interest

Where a Member's comment or vote materially favours or disfavours its own product, service, or commercial position, the Member must declare the interest in the record. The declaration attaches to the comment or vote for the life of the WG's records.

## 11. Security

Members must apply security controls appropriate to the sensitivity of WG Personal Data, including:

- access on a need-to-know basis to named personnel;
- authentication with at least industry-standard controls (multi-factor authentication on any account with access);
- encryption in transit and at rest;
- logging of access sufficient to support incident investigation;
- deletion of local copies when no longer needed for a permitted purpose (§6), subject to §13 retention.

## 12. Incident notification

A Member that becomes aware of a personal-data breach affecting WG Personal Data must notify the Convener **without undue delay, and in any event within 48 hours** of becoming aware.

**Rationale.** The 48-hour internal window sits inside GDPR Article 33's 72-hour external window. It gives the Convener a working 24 hours to assess whether the breach is notifiable to a supervisory authority, prepare notification, and coordinate any communication to affected reviewers. This approach is consistent with the practice of open-standards projects operating under a foundation umbrella; the shorter internal window is a coordination mechanism, not a substitute for each controller's independent statutory duties.

Notification must include the nature of the breach, categories and approximate numbers of individuals affected, likely consequences, and measures taken or proposed. Nothing in this section limits a Member's own notification obligations under applicable law; each joint controller retains its own statutory reporting duty.

## 13. Retention

- **Reviewer Data (comment content and metadata):** retained by Members as part of their WG participation record for the life of the protocol version to which it relates, plus five (5) years. Personal identifiers may be pseudonymised earlier at the Member's discretion.
- **WG Data (Member personnel):** retained per each Member's internal records-retention policy.
- **Correspondence:** twenty-four (24) months from close of the matter, unless a longer period is required by law.

Substantive comments (with reviewer identifiers pseudonymised where the reviewer chose pseudonymous or anonymous submission) may be retained indefinitely as part of the published protocol record.

## 14. International transfers

Where WG Personal Data is transferred between jurisdictions, the transferring party will rely on:

- an adequacy decision;
- Standard Contractual Clauses (EU/UK) or an equivalent approved mechanism;
- another lawful transfer mechanism.

The Convener will maintain and publish the list of transfer mechanisms in force for the central WG operations. Members are responsible for the mechanisms covering their own transfers.

## 15. Data-subject rights

Where a reviewer exercises a right against a Member directly, the Member will:

- respond in accordance with the applicable law and Schedule 1 §4;
- notify the Convener of the request and outcome if it affects the WG's published record (for example, a request for erasure of a published comment);
- coordinate with the Convener where an erasure or restriction request cannot be honoured on public-interest or freedom-of-expression grounds (GDPR Art. 17(3)).

## 16. Term, withdrawal, survival

This Charter takes effect on the date the Member is admitted to the WG and continues for the duration of the Member's participation. On withdrawal or removal:

- §§ 5–9, 11–15, 17, 18, 20 and Schedule 1 survive in respect of WG Personal Data received during participation;
- the Member must within thirty (30) days confirm to the Convener whether it will retain, return, or delete WG Personal Data in its custody, and on what basis;
- obligations that survive continue in favour of the Successor Foundation on matriculation.

## 17. Governing law and dispute resolution

This Charter is governed by the laws of the DIFC. Disputes will be referred to the DIFC Courts. Nothing in this section limits a data subject's right to bring proceedings before a supervisory authority or court of competent jurisdiction under the applicable data-protection law.

## 18. Order of precedence

If this Charter conflicts with any other WG document, the order of precedence is: (i) applicable law; (ii) this Charter; (iii) the WG Terms of Participation; (iv) the APP Contribution Terms; (v) other WG documents.

## 19. Amendment

The Interim Custodian, and after matriculation the Successor Foundation, may propose amendments. A material amendment requires the consent of Members representing at least two-thirds of the WG. The Convener will publish the amended Charter and its effective date. Amendments must not diminish reviewer protections.

## 20. Assignment on matriculation

Each Member consents in advance to assignment of this Charter to the Successor Foundation on matriculation, with all rights and obligations continuing unchanged. No further Member signature is required to give effect to the assignment.

## Schedule 1 — Article 26 Arrangement (Joint Controller Allocation)

This Schedule allocates responsibility for GDPR compliance between the Convener and Members for the joint processing described in Charter §5. Nothing here limits a reviewer's right under GDPR Article 26(3) to exercise their rights against any joint controller.

### 1. Transparency (Articles 13–14)

- **Convener** — maintains and publishes the APP consultation privacy notice, including the essence of this Arrangement. Ensures the notice is presented to reviewers at the point of submission.
- **Members** — may not issue conflicting transparency information regarding the joint processing without the Convener's agreement. Members remain responsible for transparency in respect of any independent processing they perform.

### 2. Lawful basis (Article 6)

- **Convener** — determines and documents the lawful basis for the joint processing (consent for submission and attribution; legitimate interests for consultation operations).
- **Members** — bound by the same lawful basis for joint processing; determine their own bases for any independent processing.

### 3. Consent management

- **Convener** — captures, stores, and honours reviewer consent (submission, attribution choice, opt-in communications). Notifies Members of any withdrawal that requires action by Members.
- **Members** — must action a withdrawal notice within thirty (30) days of receipt from the Convener.

### 4. Data-subject rights (Articles 15–22)

- **Central point of contact:** the Convener, at the address published in the privacy notice.
- **Convener** — first-line handler of all reviewer requests, coordinates with Members where the request affects records held by Members.
- **Members** — respond to requests received directly, notify the Convener, and cooperate with a coordinated response where required.
- **Response timeline:** one month from receipt by any joint controller, extendable per Article 12(3).

### 5. Records of processing (Article 30)

- Each joint controller maintains its own Article 30 records for its role in the joint processing.

### 6. DPIA (Article 35)

- **Convener** — conducts and maintains the DPIA for the consultation infrastructure and joint processing. Shares outcomes with Members.
- **Members** — conduct their own DPIAs for independent processing where required.

### 7. Breach notification (Articles 33–34)

- Each joint controller retains its own supervisory-authority notification duty.
- Internal notification cascade per Charter §12.
- **Convener** — leads communication to affected reviewers (Article 34) where a communication is required and the breach affects central infrastructure or reviewer contact details. Members lead communications only for breaches confined to their own systems, coordinating with the Convener.

### 8. International transfers (Chapter V)

- Each joint controller is responsible for the transfer mechanism covering its own transfers of WG Personal Data. The Convener maintains the register of central mechanisms per Charter §14.

### 9. Sub-processors and onward recipients

- Members must not appoint sub-processors to process WG Personal Data received under the joint processing without prior notice to the Convener.
- Onward disclosure is limited by Charter §7.

### 10. Records of joint decisions

- The Convener maintains a record of joint decisions affecting the processing (for example, changes to retention, new categories of recipient, amendments to this Arrangement).

### 11. Liability as between joint controllers

- As between the joint controllers, and without prejudice to Article 82(4), each is responsible for damages arising from its own non-compliance. Where damage arises from a joint controller acting outside the scope of this Charter or in breach of its terms, that controller indemnifies the others for the resulting liability, subject to Article 82(5).

### 12. Review

- The Convener reviews this Arrangement annually and on any material change to the WG's activities, and publishes any amendment as an updated essence-of-arrangement in the privacy notice.
